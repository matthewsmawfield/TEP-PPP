Abstract

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) predicts that proper time behaves as a dynamical scalar field, producing spatially correlated fluctuations in distributed clock networks. Papers 1–3 established persistent, distance-structured clock correlations across 364–539 GNSS stations and up to 1.17 billion station-pair measurements, identifying a spatial correlation length $\lambda_T \approx 4{,}200$ km in receiver clock residuals. This study extends the detection onto independent satellite-pair and optical-clock channels, separates the signal from its leading geodetic and instrumental confounds, and converts the spatial covariance into a deployment-ready navigation and timing prior.

In the cross-spectral phase-alignment observable $\cos(\arg \sum_f S_{ij}(f))$, precise receiver clocks recover $\lambda = 4{,}348$ km on COD and $4{,}076$ km on GFZ ($R^2 = 0.898$ and $0.955$, $25$–$27\sigma$ above phase-randomised nulls), while orbiting satellite clock pairs — sharing no ground receiver and no common-view geometry — exhibit distance-structured phase alignment at $10.6$–$15.9\sigma$ and $26.4\sigma$ on a GPS×Galileo cross-constellation arm ($\lambda = 8{,}140 \pm 795$ km, a band-membership statement within a shared analysis-centre adjustment rather than an independent recovery of the ground scale). On the ground, two-stage panel regression separates the zero-shared-satellite intercept from co-visibility ($\mathrm{PC} = -0.164$ at $10.8\sigma$, strengthening to $-0.267$ with solar-time control), preserves the $500$–$800$ km East–West anisotropy ($+0.130 \rightarrow +0.126$), and discriminates the Cosmic Microwave Background dipole axis under sub-diurnal Earth-rotation aperture synthesis (winning $300/300$ day-clustered bootstrap draws). Oscillator-class stratification shows that idiosyncratic noise is identical between quartz and atomic stations ($0.99\times$) whereas network-coupled coherent power is $3.01\times$ larger on quartz ($7.37\times$ in station-median LF coherent RMS, $p = 0.0273$), isolating lunar $M_2$ tidal phase coupling to metadata-verified quartz clocks (dependence-preserving joint COD+GFZ station-permutation $z_{\mathrm{joint}} = +5.73\sigma, p_{\mathrm{joint}} = 0.0003$, conditional Fisher $p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 1.9\times 10^{-6}$, vs atomic $z_{\mathrm{joint}} = -0.87\sigma, p_{\mathrm{joint}} = 0.374$, Fisher $0.55$); antenna model organises per-station broadband coupling at least as strongly as oscillator class ($\eta^2 = 0.37$ vs $0.30$), so the oscillator-class signature is retained at lunar $M_2$ and in the co-located differentials).

Out-of-ecosystem reanalysis of the 65-day 2023 European optical-fibre clock campaign across NPL, LNE-OP/SYRTE, PTB, and INRIM constrains differential frequency-ratio offsets across the Doppler-cancelled NPL–PTB link to $-7.1 \pm 7.7\times 10^{-18}$ ($1\sigma$; conditional $95\%$ single-effect envelope $\le 2.2\times 10^{-17}$, with $1$ s 3-node star-tree closure at $3.61\times 10^{-20}$ on the $b_1 = 0$ tree), resolves an excess sub-mHz floor of $\tau_{\mathrm{DL}} = 8.68$–$27.92\times 10^{-18}$ on remote links (largely attributable to clock-specific terms; co-located floor $0.00\times 10^{-18}$ at NPL vs $7.86$ and $24.76\times 10^{-18}$ at PTB; 4-clock baseline excess $7.62$–$7.74\times 10^{-18}$ on $212{,}605$ simultaneous $1$ s common epochs and $7.30\times 10^{-18}$ full-campaign; 8-pair $\rho = +0.927, p = 9.4\times 10^{-4}$, 4-group $\rho_{\mathrm{group}} = +0.800$, exact one-sided $p = 0.167$) alongside $705$ km lunar $M_2$ amplitude enhancement (Fisher $p = 0.0014$ raw, $0.0019$ demeaned; joint species-frame $\mathrm{Yb^+\,E3}-\mathrm{Sr}$ simultaneous phase clustering $R = 0.990, p_{\mathrm{joint,6h}} = 0.0230$ vs $R = 0.477$ for four geographic ratios on a separate window ($R = 0.358$ for the same three simultaneous ratios) in the geographic frame), detects zero-fibre $705$ km anti-phase coherence probing a non-universal $\Delta K_\alpha$ electromagnetic coupling extension between co-located optical pairs at NPL and PTB ($\mathrm{PA} = -0.335 \pm 0.091, z = -3.69, z_{\mathrm{null}} = -3.27, p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 0.0010$), and localises the zero-satellite optical clock pairs globally to $879$–$1{,}416$ km against the 366-station GNSS archive (conditional two-site Fisher $p = 0.0030$; shared GNSS reference; sub-diurnal $f > 30\,\mu\text{Hz}$ error $704$–$1{,}285$ km).

Operationally, a phase-resolved TEP clock prior requires no new hardware and under 1 ms per epoch on existing CORS/RTK networks: (i) for timing holdover, it reduces sky-outage clock-residual error by $38.1\%$ at 1 h ($24.7$ ns) and $55.7\%$ at 8 h ($65.6$ ns), outperforming datasheet holdover of a $\sim\$300$ OCXO beyond 30 min and a $\sim\$2{,}000$ Rubidium standard beyond 2 h; (ii) for outage bridging, it reduces clock RMS by $5.0$–$18.9\%$ over 90 EUREF days and beats every deployable baseline on held-out 2025 across Europe, North America, and East Asia; and (iii) for degraded-geometry positioning, it restores $k = 3$ availability in single-epoch code SPP at parity with a conventional AR(1) clock prior ($5.76$ m vs $5.53$ m RMS) and yields a $+29.6\%$ 3-D carrier-phase RMSE reduction, improves $k = 4$–$5$ 3-D RMS by $+14.5$–$48.0\%$, localises co-located GNSS anchors to $339$ km (Matera) and $744$ km (Tahiti) and 43 live NTRIP stations to $191.8$ km median error, and achieves $+35.84\%$ mean two-hour holdover gain across 185 walk-forward windows on a 68-station live observatory ($116$ of $120$ SHA-256-sealed ex-ante envelopes positive, $+29.01\%$ mean on overlapping sliding windows; zero-target-overlap clean-origin forward component $+2.57\%, p = 1.8\times 10^{-5}$).

Keywords: Temporal Equivalence Principle; GNSS atomic clocks; Precise Point Positioning (PPP); spatial clock priors; GNSS outage bridging; timing holdover; cross-spectral phase alignment; cryptographically sealed evaluation
