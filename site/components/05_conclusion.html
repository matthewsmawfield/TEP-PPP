5. Conclusion

This study independently reproduces the predicted GNSS clock correlation scale with the corrected Papers 1–2 phase-alignment estimator: $\lambda=4{,}348$ km on COD and $4{,}076$ km on GFZ precise receiver clocks ($R^2=0.898$ and $0.955$, 25–27$\sigma$ above phase-randomised nulls), within 3.5% of the foundational exponential $1/e$ measurement. The detection holds on a channel whose pairwise statistic shares no receiver clocks and no common-view geometry with the original measurements: orbiting satellite clocks show the same distance-structured phase alignment at $10.6$–$15.9\sigma$ above phase-randomised and geometry-shuffled nulls, with a GPS×Galileo cross-constellation arm — sharing no clock family or orbital plane, though estimated through the same analysis-centre tracking segment as the receiver products — recovering a $26.4\sigma$ log-slope ($-0.172$) and $+0.30$ near/far contrast ($\lambda = 8{,}140 \pm 795$ km pooled over 180 days; $8{,}140$–$14{,}911$ km across 90-day windows due to long-baseline pedestal trade-off, Step 178). On the ground, the non-common-view intercept separates from satellite co-visibility at the pre-registered criterion on the minimally processed broadcast product ($\mathrm{PC} = -0.164$, $10.8\sigma$, strengthening to $-0.267$ with the local-solar-time systematic controlled; out-of-sample $R^2$ gain $+0.022$; raw intercept fit $\lambda = 4{,}349$ km, and $\lambda = 1{,}008$ km at $6.5\sigma$ after projecting out the collinear co-visibility and solar-time profiles), the predicted East–West anisotropy at $500$–$800$ km survives common-view removal essentially unchanged ($+0.130 \rightarrow +0.126$, $z = +1.98 \rightarrow +1.91$), oscillator-class dependence separates field coupling from local noise (idiosyncratic $0.99\times$, network-coupled $3.01\times$ on quartz and $7.37\times$ in station-median LF coherent RMS at exact station-permutation $p = 0.0273$, persisting on co-located receivers that share site and sky, isolating lunar $M_2$ coupling to metadata-verified quartz clocks at $p_{\mathrm{perm}} = 1.9\times10^{-6}$ vs atomic $0.55$, and yielding a double dissociation between the atmospheric $S_2$ and gravitational $M_2$ tidal lines), and sub-diurnal aperture synthesis identifies the field's preferred axis with the CMB dipole at $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} = -2{,}315.5$ (winning 300 of 300 day-clustered bootstrap draws and both disjoint half-year splits).

The operational payoffs are quantified and deployable on existing CORS/SBAS/RTK infrastructure with no new hardware and sub-millisecond per-epoch computation. During a sky-view outage the network prediction bounds a receiver's clock-residual error to 25 ns at 1 h and 66 ns at 8 h — a network-assisted residual bound, not an absolute UTC error against a traceable hardware reference, and the receiver depends on live network information rather than operating autonomously; compared on datasheet drift terms this outperforms a $\sim\$300$ GNSS-disciplined OCXO beyond 30 min and a $\sim\$2{,}000$ Rubidium beyond 2 h, while a $\sim\$5$ TCXO with network aiding achieves 146$\times$ better residual bound than free-running at 1 h. Across 90 days on a dense EUREF network, the fixed kernel reduces clock RMS by $5.0$–$18.9\%$ versus the strongest temporal predictor at 10 min–8 h outages, and the phase-resolved variant adds a further $2.6$–$17.1\%$ over the distance-only kernel; frozen on 2024, the full-band construction beats every deployable baseline on held-out 2025 across three coherent networks. With target coordinates withheld, the 43-mode phase fingerprint localises co-located GNSS anchors globally to 339 km (Matera) and 744 km (Tahiti), localises the zero-satellite co-located optical clock pairs at PTB and NPL globally to $879$–$1{,}416$ km against the 366-station GNSS archive (conditional two-site Fisher $\chi^2_4 = 16.00, p = 0.0030$; 3-pair $p = 3.1\times 10^{-4}$), and localises 43 live NTRIP stations to a 191.8 km median error on enclosed stations (214.7 km across all 43) from 30 minutes of clock data alone. At three visible satellites, where free-clock SPP is algebraically infeasible, an external clock prior restores a bounded fix (5.76 m RMS, 95.2% availability in single-epoch code SPP, at pseudorange-noise-limited parity with a conventional AR(1) clock prior at 5.53 m), and under carrier-phase tracking the causal network prior beats persistence by $+29.6\%$ in unlatched 3-D position error ($+46.7\%$ vertical); at $k = 4$–$5$ it improves 3-D RMS by $+14.5$–$48.0\%$ over persistence-trend. A 68-station live observatory confirms the result in real time ($+35.84\%$ mean two-hour holdover gain across 185 walk-forward windows, $p = 2.15\times10^{-50}$; 275 of 276 recorded live archives positive over $95.6$ h at $+42.93\%$ mean), and 120 cryptographically sealed weight envelopes score their first post-seal windows at $+29.01\%$ mean (116 of 120 positive) under SHA-256 verification across a 122-link hash-chained anchor log.

The bounds are stated equally plainly. With a full sky the receiver clock is already observable and the prior is correctly inert; at $k = 3$ in single-epoch code SPP the pseudorange noise floor dominates, so TEP supplies the missing degree of freedom at parity with the best conventional clock prior rather than exceeding it. The sealed walk-forward figure decomposes into verified retrodiction of member-observed epochs plus a spatially specific forward component at never-observed epochs ($+2.57\%$, $p = 1.8\times10^{-5}$, under causal penalty selection; $+3.39\%$ untapered, $z = +5.8$ to $+6.6$ above station-label permutation, frozen under prospective preregistration), bounded by a measured $\sim$90-minute frozen-weight coherence time that fixes the deployed refit cadence. The claim is confined to receivers whose displacement during an outage is small compared to $\lambda_T$: on the GRACE-FO Ultra-Stable Oscillator at 7.5 km/s the prior loses to persistence by $16$–$46\%$, and the Flight 8 UAV archive exercises sub-minute extrapolation on a steered TCXO, a regime in which TEP makes no prediction. Together, these results establish a multi-channel observational case for geographically structured clock behaviour across GNSS receiver and inter-satellite clock products, strengthened by independently acquired European optical-clock measurements and cryptographically sealed operational prediction tests, with the pre-registered benchtop quartz experiment still awaiting independent hardware data: the combined spatial, phase, and oscillator-dependent signatures are consistent with the proposed TEP phenomenology and provide multiple discriminating tests of its physical interpretation — recovering the predicted correlation scale on an independent satellite-pair geometry, separating from the leading instrumental confound at a pre-registered threshold, carrying the predicted anisotropy and celestial axis, unmasking the sub-mHz floor and candidate non-universal $\Delta K_\alpha$ anti-phase coherence on optical clocks, and predicting unscored data under cryptographic seal — while the specified station-identity, common-mode, geometry, phase-randomisation and sealed-envelope controls do not reproduce the observed signal. The temporal structure that the GNSS literature treats as noise is spatially coherent, predictable, and operationally exploitable; the infrastructure to exploit it is already deployed.
